## Validated learnings (carry these forward)

- **Real, specific, named proof beats composite/anonymized proof** — but only on
  first use. Reusing the same proof story a 2nd or 3rd time has coincided with
  the account's worst results twice (post 11, post 15) — treat reused proof as
  a red flag, not a shortcut.
- **Client-story structure (problem → intervention → outcome) is the strongest
  proven pattern** — posts 3 and 8 both did well with it (2 supporting data
  points, upgraded to "Strong" before retirement).
- **Honest-failure / "here's what broke" postmortems have underperformed twice**
  (posts 9 and 22 — the account's two lowest results), even with real proof and
  on-brand visuals. Avoid this angle until there's a reason to retest it cleanly.
- **Stat-first / news-lead hooks have coincided with weak results twice**
  (posts 11 and 15) versus specific-fact/personal-action hooks which produced
  the top 2 results (posts 1 and 2). Not proven causal, but worth defaulting
  away from.
- **Heavy hashtag blocks (9-12 tags) and off-brand visuals both track with the
  lowest-performing posts** (4, 5, 6). Zero or near-zero hashtags plus on-brand
  dark-teal visuals track with the stronger half.
- **Engagement rate and reach don't always move together** — post 12 had the
  account's best engagement rate (~6%) on modest reach with no proof; don't
  chase impressions alone.
- **A post needs one single, specific, checkable, fresh artifact at its
  center** — the clearest single differentiator across the full 15-post sample.
